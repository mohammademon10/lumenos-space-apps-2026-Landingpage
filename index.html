The Vacuum Paradox

Heat in a vacuum: No air, no fans.

In orbit, convection is impossible. Heat can only escape as infrared radiation via panels. How fast heat radiates depends strictly on your View Factor: what your radiator sees.

The Thermodynamic Brain

How LumenOS thinks: The 3 conditions.

Terrestrial operating systems schedule jobs based on CPU load and RAM availability. LumenOS schedules based on orbit phase, thermal absorption, and radiator view.

Condition A 🚀

Sunlight + Deep Space

Solar arrays deliver peak generation (up to 2,000 W) while radiators face 3 Kelvin deep space. Maximum cooling headroom is unlocked.

Action: Run Heavy AI Workloads (LLM Fine-Tuning & Model Training).
Condition B ⚠️

Radiator Facing Earth

Earth-day albedo radiates 280 K infrared back into the satellite. Radiator cooling plummets to ~491 W. Heavy workloads would trigger a thermal runaway.

Action: Checkpoint Heavy jobs, pause safely at 56°C; dispatch Medium Workloads.
Condition C 🌑

Orbital Eclipse

Satellite traverses Earth's shadow for ~35 minutes. Solar power is 0 W; satellite operates on its 500 Wh battery pack (capped at 200 W discharge).

Action: Deep Sleep compute; execute Cold Workloads (sorting, log compaction).
